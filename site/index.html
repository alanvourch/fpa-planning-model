Alan Vourc'h · FP&A, planning and scenarios

Hire in phases, commit the cloud, and hold the bigger plan until retention proves out.

That is what this planning model tells the CFO and the head of engineering of a USD 24M ARR software company to do over the next 18 months. It reaches USD 40M of ARR by February 2028 in the base case, keeps at least 23 months of cash runway even in the downside, and holds gross margin above 74%. The front-loaded alternative buys USD 1.7M more ARR and breaks the runway guardrail.

1 · The decision

One question, three options, two guardrails

Northlight is a fictional but realistic Series C software company: two products, 142 people in Montréal, Paris and Austin, growing 41% a year and burning USD 762k a month. Cloud hosting has climbed from 7.9% of revenue in February to 9.3% in August. The question every scale-up CFO gets asked: how much to hire, where, and what to commit on cloud, without running out of cash or margin.

Option

Hold hiring

Backfills only. Ends with USD 21.4M of cash and USD 38.3M of ARR in the base case, USD 2.2M below the recommended plan.

Recommended

Phased plan

22 hires from October 2026 to May 2027, 14 of them in R&D. USD 40.5M of ARR in the base case; runway never below 23 months in the downside.

Fails a guardrail

Front-loaded plan

31 hires by early 2027 plus product contractors. Only USD 1.7M more ARR than the phased plan, and downside runway falls to 16.9 months, 1.1 short of the floor.

On cloud, the model is unambiguous. Running on demand fails the gross margin floor (the base case bottoms at 72.9%). A one-year commitment of 444,820 compute units a month at a 25% discount saves USD 0.8M over 18 months and lifts the minimum margin by 2.0 points. The cost of that insurance in the downside: USD 178k of committed units nobody uses.

What a company does differently because of this.

  • Engineering opens 14 requisitions in a fixed order over eight months instead of 18 inside a quarter, and the Insights contractor push waits for the Q1 2027 retention review.
  • The cloud commitment is signed before the renewal window, sized at 85% of expected usage so the downside does not strand much.
  • Two guardrails become standing policy: 18 months of downside runway and a 74% gross margin floor, checked every month against the same drivers.

One senior software engineer, first-year cost, from the salary and location tables. Employer tax rates are derived from the 2026 Québec, French and United States rate tables.

Loaded cost = salary plus 8% bonus, employer tax on both, and benefits. Recruiting adds 15% of salary once; equipment adds USD 3,500 to cash. A start month later means exactly one month less of all of it, which the tests check.

Revenue is capacity, not a growth rate

New customers a month = ramped account executives × customers per ramped AE × seasonality. An AE hired in January sells nothing that month, a quarter of quota in February, and full quota from May. Churn and expansion come from gross and net retention; the January price increase is its own line; Insights revenue is customers × attach rate × usage.

Cloud cost = compute units × unit price, with committed units at a discount and overage at list. Customer success hires are added automatically when coverage drops below 45 customers per manager.

6 · Verify it

Run it, read the tests, open the workbook

Python 3.12, pandas, numpy, openpyxl, matplotlib. No language model anywhere, no dashboard tool, no build step.

git clone https://github.com/alanvourch/fpa-planning-model.git
cd fpa-planning-model
python -m venv .venv
.venv/Scripts/pip install -r requirements.txt
.venv/Scripts/python.exe run_model.py
.venv/Scripts/python.exe -m pytest -q

The run regenerates the dataset, the budget, the bridge, the 18 scenario runs, the Excel workbook, the memo, the board pack and this page. Two runs give identical files.

What the tests hold

  • Reconciliation. ARR roll-forward, P&L subtotals, product P&L, payroll to the workforce table, cloud rule, and cash roll-forward, for every one of the 18 runs plus actuals and budget.
  • Workforce. A hire costs exactly its loaded salary from its start month, merit applies each January, an end date stops cost, an AE adds customers only after ramp, and one extra engineer moves EBITDA and cash by exactly the expected amount.
  • Bridge. Components tie to each line and to EBITDA every month, and recover the six planted deviations.
  • Excel. Values match the Python output, and Excel itself recalculates every formula to the same figures.
  • Documents. Every number in the memo and on this page traces to a model output.
  • Adversarial. See the repository's decisions file for what the adversarial test caught.

The Excel export

northlight_planning_model.xlsx: assumptions with sources, the change log, actuals, budget, the bridge, the recommended plan's three scenarios with the ARR roll-forward, cloud and cash, the workforce by function and location, and a Checks sheet where every subtotal is an Excel formula recomputed against the model value. Yellow cells are model values, blue cells are formulas.

What the model deliberately does not do

  • It does not model interest, income tax, depreciation, capitalized development or foreign exchange. EBITDA is the operating line and cash follows it.
  • The actuals are synthetic and generated by the same engine, so the bridge reconciles exactly by construction. Real ledgers need a reconciliation tolerance and an unallocated line.
  • Plan headcount carries attrition as an expected vacancy allowance, not as simulated leavers.
  • Marketing spend scales with the new-customer target; it does not drive it.
  • 35 of the 39 drivers are judgment calls, labelled as such in the driver table above. The benchmark-anchored ones cite the survey they come from.
