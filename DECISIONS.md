# Decisions

One entry per significant modelling or design choice: what was chosen, why,
and what was rejected. Written during the build (September 2026) and kept
for interview use.

## Scope and positioning

- **Company: a fictional B2B SaaS scale-up, "Northlight Software".** Series C,
  about USD 24M ARR at August 2026, about 140 people across Montréal (HQ,
  engineering, G&A), Paris (engineering) and Austin (go-to-market). Reporting
  currency USD, salaries stated in USD equivalents. Rejected: a Canadian-dollar
  reporting entity, which would have added a planning-rate layer without
  adding to the decision.
- **One executive question, one decision engine.** The model answers "how
  should the CFO and R&D leader allocate headcount, cloud and vendor spend and
  product investment over 18 months while protecting gross margin and cash
  runway" by evaluating a small grid of options (three hiring plans crossed
  with cloud on demand versus a one-year commitment) under three scenarios,
  applying two guardrails, and choosing by a stated objective. Rejected: a
  free-form scenario tool with no recommendation, because the brief asks
  for a memo that states a decision.
- **No LLM anywhere.** The neighbouring close-automation project already
  shows AI agents. This one proves the finance machinery is hand-built.
- **No Power BI, no dashboards.** A single self-contained HTML page with
  inline SVG, matching the neighbouring project's showcase page.

## Modelling choices

- **Revenue recognized as closing ARR divided by 12.** Simple, auditable,
  and the ARR roll-forward (opening, new, expansion, price, churn) is the
  primary object. Rejected: average of opening and closing, which hides the
  roll-forward behind an interpolation.
- **Net revenue retention is all-in.** Benchmarks quote NRR including price
  increases, so the January price step is carried as its own bucket and the
  monthly expansion rate is derived from (NRR minus price) and GRR. An
  earlier draft stacked the price step on top of NRR and produced 45% growth
  from a 105% NRR assumption, which the benchmark does not support.
- **New logos come from ramped sales capacity, not from a typed growth
  rate.** New customers per month equal ramped account-executive equivalents
  times logos per ramped AE times a seasonality index. Hiring an AE therefore
  changes revenue only after the ramp, which is what the brief means by
  hiring decisions flowing through correctly.
- **Marketing programme spend scales with the new-logo target** (cost per
  new logo). Rejected: a fixed programme budget, which would not move when the
  sales plan moves.
- **Insights add-on modelled as attach rate times revenue per customer.**
  Cloud usage per Insights customer is a separate driver, so a data-heavy
  add-on can dilute gross margin, which is the real tension in the decision.
- **Cloud cost is usage times unit price under a commitment rule.** Committed
  units are priced at a discount, overage at list. The commitment is sized
  once (coverage times the average base-scenario units of the phased plan
  without commitment) and applied to every option and scenario, so the
  downside shows stranded commitment honestly.
- **Workforce: seats with start and end months.** Salary, bonus, employer
  tax and benefits are computed per seat per month from the salary and
  location tables; merit applies every January after the start month;
  recruiting cost and equipment capex hit once, in the start month. Employer
  tax rates are derived from published 2026 rate tables (Québec, France,
  United States) and labelled as such.
- **Attrition: real end dates in actuals, a vacancy allowance in plans.**
  Plans carry expected leavers times backfill lag as a negative fractional
  seat by cost bucket and location. Rejected: simulating leavers in the plan,
  which would make plan headcount random and the bridge noisy.
- **Customer success hiring is driver-based.** CSMs are added automatically
  when ramped capacity falls below customers divided by customers per CSM.
  One extra pass suffices because CSMs do not change revenue.
- **Cash = EBITDA minus capex plus working capital.** Deferred revenue is
  the annual-upfront share of ARR times 5.5/12 (uniform renewal dates);
  receivables are revenue times DSO/30; financing is a separate line. No
  interest, tax, depreciation or capitalized development. Runway is closing
  cash divided by the trailing three-month average net burn.
- **Product P&L stops at gross profit.** Opex is by function only.
  Allocations: cloud by units, everything else in COGS by revenue share.

## Actuals, budget and bridge

- **Actuals are generated by the same engine from realized drivers.** Six
  deviations from budget are planted (sales productivity, retention, price,
  Insights usage, late engineering hires, programme overspend). The bridge
  is therefore exact by construction, and the README says so.
- **The FY2026 budget is a frozen assumption snapshot (v1.0) plus its own
  hiring plan**, run from the December 2025 closing position. The change
  log carries every value that moved since, and a test replays the log onto
  the snapshot and requires it to equal the current file.
- **Bridge decomposition.** Revenue: volume and price per product. Cloud:
  usage, rate, fixed. Payroll: headcount and rate per function. Everything
  else: spend. Impacts are stated as effect on EBITDA, favorable positive,
  and must sum to the EBITDA variance to the cent, per month.
- **Materiality: a dual test (absolute and percentage of the budget line)
  or an absolute override**, the same shape as the neighbouring project.

## Calibration (recorded so nobody mistakes it for fitting to a conclusion)

- First run: hold option was cash-positive and growth about 45% a year;
  every option passed both guardrails, so the decision had no content.
  Changes, in order: a smaller opening revenue base and fewer account
  executives; NRR treated as all-in; G&A fixed and rent reduced toward the
  benchmark share; downside retention and productivity moved to the low end
  of the benchmark range. Each change is a driver, not an output.

## What the adversarial tests caught (tests/test_adversarial.py, tests/test_documents.py)

- **Typed figures in the renderers.** A regex sweep of every quoted string in
  `model/reports.py` and `build_site.py` for numbers that look like model
  figures found two real ones on the first run: the cloud commitment coverage
  written as `85%` into slide 5 of the board pack, and the price increase
  written as "6% against 5%" into slide 2. Both now come from the facts
  dictionary (the coverage from the assumption table, the two price steps from
  the realized drivers and the v1.0 budget snapshot). The sweep also flagged a
  hex colour code, which was a false positive and is now excluded.
- **Untraceable residual on the page.** The page tracer, which requires every
  number in the rendered HTML to exist in the facts dictionary, found the
  "All other" bucket of the bridge waterfall (USD 128k) being computed inside
  the page builder. It is now a fact (`bridge_other`) shared by the page and
  the board pack.
- **The start-shift test caught my own arithmetic first.** Pushing every
  planned hire one month later must remove exactly one seat-month per seat.
  The first version of the test expected the lost month to carry a January
  merit increase; the engine (correctly) removes the start month, which has
  none. The engine was right and the test was wrong; the test now also checks
  that the vacancy allowance shrinks in proportion and that recruiting and
  equipment costs move rather than vanish.
- **Known looseness of the number tracer.** The allowed set admits twelve
  renderings of every fact (raw, thousands, millions, percent, at zero to two
  decimals) and every numeric token in the assumption, salary and change-log
  tables, and axis tick labels are exempt. A small typed integer such as 12 or
  45 could therefore pass by coincidence. The typed-figure sniff on the
  renderer source is the second net for exactly that case.
- **The guardrail responds.** Loosening the runway floor to 6 months in
  memory switches the recommendation to the front-loaded plan; tightening it to
  40 months switches it to holding. The recommendation is computed, not typed.

## Deliverables

- **Excel export with live formulas for subtotals and checks.** Leaf driver
  values are written by Python; subtotals, margins, the cash roll-forward and
  a Checks sheet are Excel formulas, so a finance reader can click through.
  A test recalculates the workbook in Excel (when installed) and compares
  every formula result to the Python output.
- **Memo and board pack generated from the outputs.** Templates in code,
  numbers from files; a test checks every figure in the memo against the
  model outputs.
- **Web page built by a script from the outputs**, with an inline-number
  tracing test, so no number on the page is typed.
